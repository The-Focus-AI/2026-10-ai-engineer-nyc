# Rank the work yourself, let an agent gather the salaries, and go talk in person

Opening the final afternoon keynote block on Day 2, Turing Post writer and founder Ksenia Se turned a five-minute host welcome into a compact workflow design exercise for conference attendees. Because two-sided hiring is one of the strongest incentives in the hallways, she proposed splitting job research cleanly between human judgment and agent legwork, and stopping short of automated resume spam.

## A human-first, agent-assisted hiring workflow

Reporting back from a day spent talking with speakers and attendees, Se observed that hiring is one of the primary reasons people attend AI Engineer, to the point that even speakers on stage are simultaneously recruiting for their teams and looking for roles themselves (00:02:12). To help attendees act on that before heading home, she sketched a four-step afternoon project (00:02:12).

First, have an agent pull the conference program into a list of speakers and their companies, then rank that list yourself by whose talks and technical work you genuinely want to build alongside (00:02:12). Next, send the agent to those companies' careers pages to match open roles to your experience and extract published compensation ranges, updating your ranking if the pay numbers change your mind (00:02:12). The crucial boundary comes at the end: never let the agent auto-submit resumes, and instead use what you learned from a speaker's talk to start a real conversation in the room (00:02:12).

**Key points**

- Hiring at the conference runs both ways, including speakers who are recruiting and job-hunting at once.
- Humans should rank companies by technical interest first; the agent then scrapes matching roles and published salary bands.
- Stop automation before submission: use talk context to start live conversations rather than auto-sending resumes.

> “The next step is to send your agent to those companies' careers pages, right, and find them open positions to match them to your experience, and collect the published compensation ranges.”
> — 00:02:12

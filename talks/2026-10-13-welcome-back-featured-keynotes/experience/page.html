Chapter 3

Four milestones of enterprise AI diffusion: 90%, 60%, 50%, 5%

Recounting a backstage conversation with conference co-organizer swyx (transcribed as "Switz"), Lieberman laid out how AI adoption drops across roughly a hundred large enterprises Tenex advises in financial services, healthcare, and industrials. He framed enterprise rollout as a four-milestone race. First, roughly 90% of those organizations have rolled out a general-purpose LLM to employees. Second, about 60% have trained staff on how to apply those interfaces in daily work.

The steeper drop arrives with autonomous workflows. Around 50% have put coding agents into active engineering and product delivery, making software teams the clear beachhead. Outside engineering, production agents working in business workloads sit at roughly 5%. Alongside open-source versus fine-tuned model choices and the difficulty of hiring AI engineers at scale, closing that 90-to-5 gap defines the work of the next 12 to 36 months before Lieberman hands the stage to Coatue's Frank Long.

“Next up is coding agents, agents that are actually used within engineering and product workflows — that's around 50% of all the enterprises we've been in, and then the final one where we are truly still day one is agents actually working in non-engineering workloads in an organization, that is around five percent right now.”00:02:29
  • Across ~100 large enterprises Tenex advises, ~90% have deployed a general-purpose LLM and ~60% have trained employees to use it.
  • Roughly 50% run coding agents inside engineering and product workflows.
  • Only ~5% run production agents in non-engineering workloads, leaving a 12-to-36-month enterprise diffusion gap.
